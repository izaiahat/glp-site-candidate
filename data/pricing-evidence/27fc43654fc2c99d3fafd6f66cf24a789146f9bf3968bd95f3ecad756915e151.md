## Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about GLP-1 + GIP Advanced

### What makes GLP-1 Advanced different from GLP-1 Core?

GLP-1 Advanced uses compounded tirzepatide, which combines both GLP-1 and GIP receptor activation for enhanced metabolic benefits. While GLP-1 Core focuses on single receptor activation, compounded tirzepatide adds GIP support for broader insulin signaling and appetite regulation. This makes GLP-1 Advanced a strong option for patients who need greater appetite suppression or have plateaued on single-receptor GLP-1 medications.

### How quickly will I see results with GLP-1 Advanced?

Most patients begin to notice early changes within 2–4 weeks, with more significant weight loss commonly occurring within the first 3 months. Because tirzepatide targets both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, many patients experience faster and more consistent progress compared to single-receptor GLP-1 options.

### What are the common side effects of tirzepatide?

Common side effects may include nausea, decreased appetite, or mild gastrointestinal discomfort, especially during dose increases. These effects often improve as the body adjusts. Your provider will monitor your response and can adjust your tirzepatide dosage to support comfort and long-term success.

### How do I know if GLP-1 Advanced is right for me?

Compounded tirzepatide may be appropriate for patients seeking stronger appetite control, improved glucose regulation, or additional support after limited results with other GLP-1 medications. A licensed provider will review your health history, goals, and lab results to determine whether a tirzepatide prescription is the right fit for you.

### What’s included when I purchase GLP-1 Advanced?

Your subscription includes 4 weeks of compounded tirzepatide medication, ongoing provider care and support, metabolic lab testing every 6 months, essential supplies (syringes and alcohol pads), wellness coaching, and full platform access with educational content.

### How do I inject my GLP-1 medication?

GLP-1 medications are administered as a subcutaneous injection, meaning the medication is injected just beneath the skin—not into muscle or vein. Most patients find the process simple and well-tolerated after their first few doses.
