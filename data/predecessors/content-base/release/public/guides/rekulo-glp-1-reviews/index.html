What Rekulo appears to be today

The store surface supports that ecommerce reading. On August 25, 2026, the captured shop said it was “Showing all 5 results” and listed creams and mullein drops across general shopping categories. This identity resolution changes the review model. A telehealth review would normally examine clinician evaluation, state availability, care cadence, prescriptions, monitoring, and a named pharmacy. Here, the current evidence supports a storefront audit first. Those clinical and pharmacy fields remain open questions rather than assumed service features.[S2][S3][S4]

Product name, ingredients, and category must stay separate

“Rekulo GLP-1” is a search phrase, not a complete product identity. A verifiable listing would need the seller’s exact product name, physical form, package quantity, volume or count, seller-listed ingredients, amounts or concentration where applicable, manufacturer or distributor, lot and expiry information, and the label category it claims. None of those target-specific fields appears in the current first-party evidence set.[S4]

Even a complete seller label would not automatically establish authoritative status. FDA explains that dietary supplements are intended to supplement the diet and can come in liquids as well as other forms. It also says a product intended to treat, diagnose, cure, or prevent disease is a drug even if labeled as a dietary supplement.[S8] That is a general category boundary, not a finding that Rekulo is either category.

FDA also says it does not approve dietary supplements for safety and effectiveness before sale, and supplement labels must list serving size, servings per container, dietary ingredients, and amounts per serving in a Supplement Facts panel.[S8] This means “FDA approved supplement” would be the wrong verification question. The useful questions are: what category does the seller claim, what does the target label state, and is there a product-specific authoritative record that matches the exact item?

For products represented as GLP-1 receptor agonist drugs, a different boundary applies. FDA says unapproved versions do not undergo its premarket review for safety, effectiveness, and quality. It recommends that patients obtain a prescription from a doctor and fill it at a state-licensed pharmacy.[S7] The captured FDA page does not mention Rekulo, so it cannot classify the missing Rekulo item. It only shows why a medicine-like name should not substitute for an exact label and dispensing record.

Do not use this checklist to self-diagnose or choose treatment. Its purpose is provenance: identifying what an item is and which entity is responsible for each claim.

Health information deserves particular caution. The public pages reviewed look like ecommerce surfaces rather than a documented clinical intake. If a later page asks about diagnoses, medications, weight, or other health details, that is a new evidence state. Prescription, pharmacy, and compounding boundaries

The current Rekulo evidence does not establish a prescription pathway, dispensing pharmacy, or compounding relationship.[S4] Do not fill those blanks from the letters “GLP-1.” If a future target listing is represented as prescription medicine, the pathway should identify the clinician, screening, prescription decision, exact product, and dispensing facility.

FDA’s current GLP-1 page lists warning signs for telehealth companies, including no screening and prescription by a licensed doctor, no licensed doctor available for questions after receipt, deep discounts that seem too good to be true, damaged or mismatched packaging, and incorrect pharmacy addresses.[S7] These are general verification prompts. They are not a Rekulo-specific conclusion.

FDA’s online-pharmacy page says likely safe signs include always requiring a doctor’s prescription, providing a US physical address and telephone number, having a licensed pharmacist available, and being licensed with a state board of pharmacy.[S9] A storefront name, warehouse, customer-service email, or shipping claim cannot replace the exact pharmacy identity. If a target ever names one, use the online GLP-1 pharmacy verification guide to check the facility and controlling board.

Compounding is also a separate status. FDA states that compounded drugs are not FDA approved and that it does not review them for safety, effectiveness, or quality before marketing.[S7] That does not mean the unseen target is compounded. It means a seller would need to state the exact product and facility before the claim could even be evaluated.
