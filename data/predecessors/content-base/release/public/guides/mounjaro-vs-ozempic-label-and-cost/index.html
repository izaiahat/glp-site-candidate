Mounjaro vs Ozempic: the short answer

Mounjaro contains tirzepatide, which acts at GIP and GLP-1 receptors. Ozempic contains semaglutide, which acts at the GLP-1 receptor. Both labels cover once-weekly prescription injections used with diet and exercise for type 2 diabetes, but the approved populations and risk-reduction indications are not identical. Neither Mounjaro nor Ozempic is FDA-approved under those brand names for weight loss. [S1][S2]

The strongest direct comparison is SURPASS-2, a 40-week open-label phase 3 trial in 1,879 adults with inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes who were taking metformin. It compared tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg with semaglutide 1 mg. That boundary matters: the trial did not compare tirzepatide with Ozempic's current 2 mg maximum dose, and an average trial result cannot identify the better medication for one person. [S1][S2][S3]

The practical answer depends on the treatment goal, current FDA indications, contraindications, tolerability, insurance rules, and the clinician's assessment. If cost is the immediate constraint, use the Mounjaro cost without insurance guide and Ozempic cost without insurance guide as separate verification starting points rather than treating list price as your expected bill.

1. They use different active ingredients

Mounjaro's active ingredient is tirzepatide. Its label describes it as an agonist at both the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide, or GIP, receptor and the glucagon-like peptide-1, or GLP-1, receptor. Ozempic's active ingredient is semaglutide, and its label describes it as a GLP-1 receptor agonist. [S1][S2]

Both products are once-weekly injections. That shared cadence can make them look interchangeable in a search result, but their active ingredients, labeled dose ranges, approved populations, and precautions are distinct. They should not be treated as generic substitutes, and this guide provides no conversion or switching instructions. [S1][S2]

For readers comparing brand boundaries, the adjacent Mounjaro versus Zepbound label-and-cost guide explains why a shared ingredient does not make two brand labels identical. The same principle applies to semaglutide brands, as outlined in the Ozempic versus Wegovy label-and-cost guide.

2. Their FDA-approved uses are not the same

Both labels include use with diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in type 2 diabetes, but their full indication sections differ. Mounjaro is labeled for adults and pediatric patients age 10 and older with type 2 diabetes. Ozempic's current label is for adults and includes glycemic control plus specified risk-reduction indications for major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease, and for sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease. [S1][S2]

This is where many comparison pages blur separate products. Evidence from Zepbound or Wegovy should not be presented as though it changed the approved Mounjaro or Ozempic brand label. This guide therefore does not import obesity-trial percentages from those brands into the direct Mounjaro-versus-Ozempic comparison.

3. The head-to-head trial favored tirzepatide—but only against semaglutide 1 mg

SURPASS-2 is more useful than comparing separate trials because it randomized participants within one study. The 40-week trial enrolled 1,879 adults with inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes who were receiving metformin. Participants received tirzepatide 5, 10, or 15 mg, or semaglutide 1 mg. It was open-label. [S1][S3]

At week 40, mean A1C changes were -2.01, -2.24, and -2.30 percentage points with tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg, compared with -1.86 percentage points with semaglutide 1 mg. Mean body-weight differences versus semaglutide favored the tirzepatide groups by 1.9, 3.6, and 5.5 kg. These are group averages from adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, not individual forecasts and not evidence that either brand is approved for weight loss. [S1][S3]

The missing 2 mg comparison is not a minor footnote. Ozempic's current label lists 0.5, 1, and 2 mg as maintenance doses based on glycemic control, while SURPASS-2 used semaglutide 1 mg. Saying only “Mounjaro beat Ozempic” strips away a key trial boundary. The accurate statement is narrower: tirzepatide at the studied doses produced larger average reductions than semaglutide 1 mg in the specified 40-week type 2 diabetes population. [S1][S2][S3]

4. The available dose ranges differ

The Mounjaro adult label spans strengths from 2.5 mg through 15 mg. It states that 2.5 mg is used for initiation and is not intended for glycemic control, and it lists 15 mg once weekly as the adult maximum. The pediatric maximum in the current label is 10 mg once weekly. Ozempic's label uses 0.25 mg for initiation and lists 0.5, 1, or 2 mg as maintenance doses based on glycemic control. [S1][S2]

Before inferring monthly cost from a sticker price, verify the prescribed presentation, package dose count, pharmacy availability, plan approval, and billing unit.

5. Side effects overlap, but label details matter

Both current labels carry a boxed warning stating that the drug caused thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents and that it is unknown whether it causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma, in humans. [S1][S2]

Both labels list contraindications involving a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2, and serious hypersensitivity to the product or its components. Those boundaries require clinician review; a comparison article cannot screen someone for them. [S1][S2]

Common gastrointestinal adverse reactions overlap. Mounjaro's label lists nausea, diarrhea, decreased appetite, vomiting, constipation, dyspepsia, and abdominal pain among reactions reported in at least 5% of treated patients. Ozempic's label lists nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, and constipation among reactions reported in at least 5% of treated patients. Different wording or percentages across labels and studies should not be simplified into a claim that one drug is universally safer or easier to tolerate. [S1][S2]

The Mounjaro label contains a specific precaution about oral hormonal contraceptives after initiation and after dose escalation. Review that instruction and the complete current medication list with a clinician or pharmacist rather than making self-directed changes. [S1]

6. List prices and what your plan can change

As of August 2, 2026, Lilly's official pricing page displayed a Mounjaro list price of $1,112.16 per fill and defined a fill as four pens. NovoCare displayed an Ozempic injection list price of $1,027.51. These are manufacturer reference prices, not a forecast of what any patient will pay. [S4][S5]

We read these list prices directly from the manufacturers' official pricing pages—Lilly for Mounjaro and NovoCare for Ozempic—on the date shown; we do not use coupon sites, aggregator price trackers, or pharmacy estimates.

The official Ozempic page did not state the package quantity or supply period represented by its displayed price, so subtracting the two figures would not be a like-for-like comparison. The right comparison is the current plan-and-pharmacy result for the exact prescription—not list price alone. [S4][S5]

7. The better fit depends on the clinical goal

The labels create different evidence lanes. A clinician considering glycemic control may weigh the SURPASS-2 findings, but must keep the semaglutide 1 mg comparator and the trial population in view. A clinician evaluating a person with established cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease may also consider the additional risk-reduction indications in Ozempic's current label. Pediatric eligibility differs because Mounjaro's label includes patients age 10 and older while Ozempic's current label is for adults. [S1][S2][S3]

Coverage can dominate an otherwise reasonable clinical comparison. Before treating either option as accessible, verify whether the plan covers the exact brand for the relevant diagnosis and what documentation is required. If coverage is absent or the claim price is not sustainable, ask the clinician and plan about lawful covered alternatives rather than relying on an unverified coupon headline.

Bottom line

Mounjaro and Ozempic share a weekly type 2 diabetes context, but they are not interchangeable. Tirzepatide produced larger average A1C and weight changes than semaglutide 1 mg in the bounded SURPASS-2 trial, while Ozempic's label includes additional cardiovascular and kidney-risk indications in specified adults. Mounjaro's label includes pediatric patients age 10 and older. [S1][S2][S3]

Use those differences to frame a clinician and insurance conversation—not to declare a universal winner. Verify the current labels, contraindications, exact pharmacy claim, and manufacturer terms before acting. Frequently asked questions

Is Mounjaro better than Ozempic?

Not universally. In SURPASS-2, tirzepatide at 5, 10, and 15 mg produced larger average A1C and body-weight changes than semaglutide 1 mg over 40 weeks in adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin. The study did not compare tirzepatide with Ozempic 2 mg and cannot predict one person's best option. [S1][S2][S3]

Are Mounjaro and Ozempic approved for weight loss?

No under these brand names. Their current labels cover type 2 diabetes indications, with additional cardiovascular and kidney-risk indications for Ozempic in specified adults. Weight change was measured in SURPASS-2, but that does not create a weight-management indication for either brand. [S1][S2][S3]

Can Mounjaro and Ozempic be taken together or switched directly?

This guide does not provide combining, conversion, or switching instructions. They are different prescription medicines with different label structures. A licensed clinician and pharmacist should assess any treatment change using the complete current labels and the person's medical context. [S1][S2]

Which costs less, Mounjaro or Ozempic?

The displayed list prices checked August 2, 2026 were $1,112.16 per four-pen Mounjaro fill and $1,027.51 for Ozempic injection. The official Ozempic page did not state the quantity or supply period behind its figure, so the two numbers are not a like-for-like comparison. List price also does not determine an individual's bill. [S4][S5]

Does Ozempic have heart and kidney indications that Mounjaro does not?

Ozempic's current label includes specified cardiovascular-risk reduction for adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease, plus specified kidney and cardiovascular-risk reduction for adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Mounjaro's current indication section does not list those same risk-reduction indications. [S1][S2]

Does the head-to-head trial compare every available dose?

No. SURPASS-2 compared tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg with semaglutide 1 mg. Ozempic's current label includes a 2 mg maximum maintenance dose, which the trial did not test. [S1][S2][S3]
