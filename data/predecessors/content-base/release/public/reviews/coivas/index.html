A consultation is a gate, not an outcome promise

Coivas’s consultation language indicates that a care-provider interaction is part of the merchant’s described pathway. It does not promise that a reader qualifies, that semaglutide will be prescribed, that compounded medication is appropriate, or that a particular result will follow. Before relying on the offer, a prospective patient can ask who conducts the consultation, what jurisdiction licenses apply, how follow-up questions are handled, which pharmacy would dispense an approved prescription, and what happens if no prescription is issued. Those are service-clarity questions, not medical advice. Product selection, prescribing, dosing, and monitoring remain matters for a licensed clinician. The practical consumer decision is whether Coivas discloses enough about the pathway and contract to justify proceeding to its consultation—not whether this page can predict treatment suitability or performance.

Clinician and prescription boundary

Coivas's published consultation requirement does not establish that any reader is eligible or that a prescription will be issued. Eligibility, prescribing, product selection, and treatment decisions belong to a licensed clinician; this review does not provide medical advice or dosing guidance.1

Compounded-drug and FDA status

The source describes compounded semaglutide, which is distinct from FDA-approved branded semaglutide drugs. Compounded drugs are not FDA approved, and FDA does not premarket-review them for safety, effectiveness, or quality.2
